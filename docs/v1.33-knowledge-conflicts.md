# Knowledge conflict engine

TuneForge now preserves reviewed disagreements between approved Guidance Claims instead of
silently selecting one source. Migration `0026` adds a reviewer-controlled conflict record and
append-only conflict events; it does not alter or remove either claim.

## Detection boundary

The automated scan compares approved claims linked to the same Canonical Setting and claim
type. It creates a `pending` candidate only when two claims have at least 60% normalized token
overlap and opposite explicit polarity. This is a discovery signal, not a truth decision.

The detector records:

- both immutable approved claim identities;
- shared canonical identities;
- evidence strength (`primary_official`, `official_manual`, or `mixed_official`);
- reviewed hardware relationship (`overlap`, `disjoint`, or `unknown`);
- reviewed BIOS relationship (`overlap`, `version_difference`, `disjoint_context`, or
  `unknown`); and
- the exact detection reason.

No automated path can mark a conflict as true. A reviewer must choose one of:

- `confirmed`: a genuine contradiction remains after scope analysis;
- `resolved_scope`: disjoint reviewed hardware explains the apparent disagreement;
- `resolved_version`: reviewed BIOS context or version explains it; or
- `dismissed`: the signal was not a meaningful disagreement.

Scope/version resolutions fail closed unless the normalized scope analysis supports the chosen
decision. Every decision requires a note and is appended to conflict history.

## User visibility

Only reviewed outcomes are public. Pending candidates and dismissed signals remain internal.
Confirmed and scope/version-resolved differences appear in citation-first Guidance and Ask
TuneForge. Both underlying claims and citations remain available; the deterministic interface
states that TuneForge has not silently reconciled them.

## Production result

The first live scan compared all 12 eligible approved-claim pairs and produced zero candidates.
That result is intentional: the engine does not create disagreements merely to populate its
queue.
