# Ask TuneForge retrieval quality

TuneForge's public answer path now distinguishes the quality and completeness of a result
without allowing a model to fill evidence gaps. The retrieval and presentation layers remain
deterministic and citation-first.

## Answer states

Reviewed guidance is labelled with one of four durable states:

- `Verified Answer`: accepted facts are available and no supplied context exposes a scope gap;
- `Partially Verified Answer`: some accepted facts were excluded or the supplied hardware/BIOS
  context is broader than the reviewed scope;
- `Conflicting Evidence`: a reviewer-confirmed conflict exists among the visible accepted facts;
  or
- `No Verified Answer`: no accepted fact is eligible for the resolved setting and context.

The public discovery surfaces retain two separate labels: `Library Match` for approved source
records and `Live Research Result` for opt-in official-site research. Neither is presented as a
reviewed answer.

## Context-aware retrieval

Each accepted fact carries explicit hardware and BIOS match status. When the user supplies a
reviewed system context, facts that do not match its hardware or BIOS version are excluded
instead of being generalized. The response reports how many facts were excluded, preserves the
scope evidence on every remaining fact, and records a refusal gap with
`applicability=scope_mismatch` when no fact remains.

The implementation reuses the existing append-only refusal reason so old databases and audit
readers remain compatible. It does not add or reinterpret knowledge.

## Presentation and resolution

Accepted claims are grouped under conservative, claim-type-derived headings:

- What it does
- What changing it does
- Available modes / constraints
- Risks / cautions

Hardware applicability and BIOS notes appear only when reviewed scopes support them. Citation
payloads retain passage, source, source-setting, manual, page, section, and content-hash
identities where available.

The question router recognizes transparent wrappers for location, safety, and available-value
questions. Near matches are shown as reviewer-approved suggestions only; typo handling never
auto-selects or creates a canonical mapping.
